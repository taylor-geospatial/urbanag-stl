St. Louis is warming at roughly 0.93 °F per decade. Climate analogs show our future looks like today's Oklahoma and Arkansas — hotter summers, shifting precipitation, and new pressures on local food production. These maps and tools help you understand where urban agriculture can work now, and how to plan for what's coming.

Why this matters

St. Louis is becoming a different climate

Annual average temperatures in St. Louis have risen steadily since the 1970s, and projections show continued warming through mid-century. The city's climate analogs — regions that today experience conditions St. Louis will face in coming decades — lie to the southwest, in states like Oklahoma, Arkansas, and Kansas.

That shift changes which crops thrive, when to plant, how much water is needed, and where the urban heat island hits hardest. Urban agriculture sited and designed for tomorrow's climate — not just today's — will be more productive and more resilient.
